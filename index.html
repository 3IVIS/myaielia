Meet Aielia — an AI assistant that takes on a whole job and sees it through: researching a list, going through your files, writing and sending the update. Most AI just wings it. Aielia follows safety rules built into its code, so it can't go off-script.

The AI suggests; the safety rules decide. We call those rules a harness. They check what Aielia may do, what it can trust, whether its answer holds up, and how much work (and money, if you set a limit) a job can use. Free and open source — and you can build your own harness.

WHAT SHE DOES

Give her the job. She works it through.

Aielia isn't just for quick questions. Give her a bigger task and she works through it step by step, using tools. At every step, the safety rules decide what she can do next.

a list job, step by step (example)
> Find the pricing page for each vendor and
  summarise the plans:
  - Acme
  - Globex
  - Initech
  - Umbrella
  - Hooli

  effort    sized from the first two vendors
  Acme      found
  Globex    found
  Initech   not found — 3 dead ends, moved on
  Umbrella  found
  Hooli     found some, ran out of effort
  reply     covers every vendor and says which
            ones are complete

> Email that summary to the team.
  Ready to send — waiting for your OK.

Work you can hand over

Research across a list

Every item gets its own search, so one dead end can't use up the effort for the rest. Each one ends as found, not found, or cut short — and the reply tells you which.

Work through your files

Let her read a folder you choose and answer questions about it. It's off until you turn it on, and only works in the terminal and desktop apps (not the browser). Any changes to your files wait for your OK.

Draft and send

She writes the message, then holds it for your OK before sending (email works in the terminal app today). Sending email, changing files and running commands never happen without your say-so.

Keep track

A to-do list of what's pending (just notes for now — it won't ping you), a plan for multi-part requests, separate threads for each goal, and a memory of what you've told her, with where each fact came from.

HOW IT STAYS SAFE

The AI suggests. The rules decide.

Instructions to an AI can get ignored. Rules built into code can't. These checks run around every turn, so Aielia stays inside the lines even when the AI slips up.

What she's allowed to do

Before she uses any tool, the rules check it: go ahead, ask you first, or don't. After mistakes, the rules get stricter.

What she trusts

Guesses are held to a higher standard. She only saves things she's very sure of, and asks you about the rest. Every fact keeps its source, clashing facts get flagged, and text from web pages is treated as information, never as orders.

Whether the answer is right

Replies are reviewed before they reach you, and partly fact-checked (2 of 9 checks run in chat today). Answers that used tools show their evidence and a clear label: verified, unverified, contradicted, or no evidence.

What a job can cost

Each item gets a limit, dead ends are spotted, big jobs ask first, and there's a hard cap on actions per turn — so a stuck job stops instead of running up the bill.

If something goes wrong

She works out what failed and picks a fix, rather than just trying again. If a job stalls, a built-in supervisor chooses what to do: change direction, look at it differently, gather more facts, or stop (asking you is possible but off by default). Progress is saved along the way, so a crash picks up where it left off.

Anything you can't take back

Changing a file, running a command or sending an email is shown to you first and only happens after you approve (unless you deliberately switch that off). A request that sounds serious gets a quick check before anything else runs.

GET STARTED

Pick the way that suits you.

In your browser

Nothing to install. Open myaielia.com/try, paste your AI key in Settings, and start chatting. The browser version is chat only — file, command and email tools are in the terminal and desktop apps.

In your terminal

curl -fsSL https://myaielia.com/install.sh | sh installs it in one step. On Windows: irm https://myaielia.com/install.ps1 | iex. Already use Node? npx @buildaharness/aielia works too. The first time you run it, it helps you pick an AI model.

As a desktop app

The same chat in its own app window, with a button to pick a folder for file access. Download for macOS, Windows or Linux, or see all downloads. The app isn't code-signed yet, so your computer may show a warning the first time you open it.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

What is Aielia?

A free AI assistant that can take on bigger jobs — researching a list, going through your files, writing and sending messages. Around the AI sits a set of safety rules (we call it a harness) that controls what it can do, what it trusts, whether its answers hold up, and how much effort or money a job can use. The AI suggests; the rules decide.

What's a harness, and why does it matter?

It's the safety layer around an AI. Telling an AI “please don't do that” can fail. A harness is code that runs on every turn, so its limits on actions, cost and trust always apply. It's the difference between an assistant that always behaves and one that usually does.

Will it act without asking me?

Only for safe things. Reading, searching and thinking happen on their own — that's the point of handing it a job. But changing a file, running a command or sending an email is shown to you first and only happens when you approve (unless you deliberately turn that off). File and command access are opt-in, and web search needs your own search key.

What if it gets stuck or fails?

It works out what went wrong and tries a fix instead of blindly repeating itself. A job that stops making progress stops itself, limits cap how much it can use, and progress is saved as it goes, so a crash picks up where it left off. Keep in mind this is an early project (v0.3.x) and hasn't had a professional security audit.

Do I need an account? Where does it run?

No account needed. You use your own AI key (Anthropic, OpenAI or OpenRouter). It runs in a browser tab, in a terminal (curl -fsSL https://myaielia.com/install.sh | sh installs it, or npx @buildaharness/aielia if you already have Node), or as a desktop app — same assistant and same safety rules in all three.

Is it free? Who makes it?

Yes — it's free under the Apache 2.0 license. Aielia is made by 3IVIS and built on Build A Harness, the open-source toolkit for designing and running AI safety rules.
