Terminal on macOS · 8 steps

  1. Step 1 of 8

    Open the Terminal app.

    Press ⌘ Space, type “Terminal” and press Enter.

  2. Step 2 of 8

    Paste this line and press Enter.

    It downloads a standalone Aielia (about 120 MB), checks its SHA-256 against the official release manifest, and installs it to ~/.local/bin/aielia. You don't need Node.

    curl -fsSL https://myaielia.com/install.sh | sh
  3. Step 3 of 8

    Check that it worked.

    You should see a version number. If you get “command not found”, add the install folder to your PATH with the second line, then close and reopen Terminal.

    aielia --version
echo 'export PATH="$HOME/.local/bin:$PATH"' >> ~/.zshrc
  4. Step 4 of 8

    Start Aielia.

    Type the command below in Terminal.

    aielia
  5. Step 5 of 8

    Paste your AI key when asked.

    The first launch asks which provider you use (Anthropic, OpenAI or OpenRouter) and for its API key. There is no Aielia account.

    Your chats are saved as files in ~/.buildaharness/personal-assistant/. File and command access stay off until you turn them on, and anything that changes something waits for your OK.

  6. Step 6 of 8

    Downloaded the file by hand? Clear the download mark.

    The installer above normally shows no warning. A file saved from a browser is different: macOS blocks it, and Terminal just prints “Killed: 9”. Run the line below on the downloaded file, then try again.

    xattr -d com.apple.quarantine ~/Downloads/aielia-darwin-*
  7. Step 7 of 8

    You're set. Update any time with one command.

    Re-running the installer also upgrades in place. To uninstall, delete the aielia program; your chats stay until you delete the folder above.

    aielia update
  8. Step 8 of 8

    Already have Node.js? You can skip the install.

    Run Aielia straight from npm instead. This is also the fallback on older Linux systems and anywhere the installer doesn't fit.

    npx @buildaharness/aielia

Desktop app on macOS · 6 steps

  1. Step 1 of 6

    Download the .dmg.

    Download Aielia {{ site.data.downloads.desktop.version }} for macOS ({{ site.data.downloads.desktop.files.dmg.sizeMB }} MB)

    It works on both Intel and Apple silicon Macs. Release notes and other files.

  2. Step 2 of 6

    Open the .dmg and drag Aielia into Applications.

    Then eject the disk image.

  3. Step 3 of 6

    Open Aielia. macOS says it “Not Opened”. That's expected.

    The message reads “Apple could not verify ‘Aielia.app’ is free of malware”, with two buttons. Click Done, not Move to Trash. The app isn't code-signed yet, so macOS can't verify it.

  4. Step 4 of 6

    Open System Settings → Privacy & Security.

    Scroll down to Security. It says “Aielia.app” was blocked to protect your Mac. Click Open Anyway, then confirm with your password or Touch ID. The button only appears after you've tried to open the app once.

  5. Step 5 of 6

    If it says “damaged” and there's no button, run this once.

    This clears macOS's “downloaded from the internet” mark from this one app. Only do it for a file you downloaded from the official Releases page. Then open Aielia again.

    xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Aielia.app
  6. Step 6 of 6

    Add your AI key in Settings.

    Click the gear icon, choose Anthropic, OpenAI or OpenRouter and paste your key. The desktop app stores it in the macOS Keychain, not in a plain file. To pick a folder Aielia may work in, use Settings → Workspace.

Desktop app on Windows · 6 steps

  1. Step 1 of 6

    Download the setup .exe.

    Download Aielia {{ site.data.downloads.desktop.version }} for Windows ({{ site.data.downloads.desktop.files.exe.sizeMB }} MB)

    Your IT team may prefer the .msi installer. Release notes and other files.

  2. Step 2 of 6

    If your browser warns about the download, choose Keep.

    Edge and Chrome warn about files they don't see often. It's expected for a new, unsigned app.

  3. Step 3 of 6

    Double-click the installer. Windows says “Windows protected your PC”.

    That's SmartScreen. It means Windows doesn't recognise the publisher yet, not that anything was found wrong. Click the small “More info” link.

  4. Step 4 of 6

    Click “Run anyway”, then follow the installer.

    The app needs Microsoft's WebView2, which is already in Windows 11 and up-to-date Windows 10. If it's missing the installer downloads it, so stay online.

  5. Step 5 of 6

    Nothing happened and no warning appeared?

    Smart App Control is probably blocking it silently. Use the browser version or the terminal install with npm instead, or switch Smart App Control off in Windows Security → App & browser control. Switching it off affects all unsigned programs and may be irreversible on older Windows 11 builds. On a work PC, your IT policy may block unsigned installers.

  6. Step 6 of 6

    Open Aielia from the Start menu and add your AI key.

    Click the gear icon, choose Anthropic, OpenAI or OpenRouter and paste your key. The desktop app stores it in a Windows-protected file, not in plain text.

Desktop app on Linux · 5 steps

  1. Step 1 of 5

    Check your Linux is recent enough.

    You need Ubuntu 22.04+, Debian 12+, Fedora or similar (64-bit Intel/AMD) with WebKitGTK 4.1. There's no security warning on Linux, nothing to bypass.

  2. Step 2 of 5

    Download the file for your distro.

    Download Aielia {{ site.data.downloads.desktop.version }} for Ubuntu / Debian ({{ site.data.downloads.desktop.files.deb.sizeMB }} MB, .deb) Download Aielia {{ site.data.downloads.desktop.version }} for Fedora ({{ site.data.downloads.desktop.files.rpm.sizeMB }} MB, .rpm) Download Aielia {{ site.data.downloads.desktop.version }} for any distro ({{ site.data.downloads.desktop.files.appimage.sizeMB }} MB, .AppImage)

    Release notes and other files.

  3. Step 3 of 5

    Install or run it.

    Use the line that matches your download, from the folder the file is in.

    # AppImage: no install needed
chmod +x Aielia_*.AppImage && ./Aielia_*.AppImage

# Ubuntu / Debian
sudo apt install ./Aielia_*.deb

# Fedora
sudo dnf install ./Aielia-*.rpm
  4. Step 4 of 5

    AppImage won't start? Install FUSE 2.

    A libfuse.so.2 error means FUSE 2 is missing. On Ubuntu 24.04 and newer the package is libfuse2t64; on older Ubuntu/Debian it's libfuse2; on Fedora, fuse-libs.

    sudo apt install libfuse2t64
  5. Step 5 of 5

    Add your AI key in Settings.

    Click the gear icon, choose Anthropic, OpenAI or OpenRouter and paste your key. It's stored through your keyring (secret-tool); if saving fails, install libsecret-tools and make sure a keyring such as GNOME Keyring is running.
